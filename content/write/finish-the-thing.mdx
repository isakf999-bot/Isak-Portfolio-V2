A site that almost works is not a site. The hover that misses on a phone, the type that jumps when a webfont lands, the form that says invalid input — those are the frames people remember.

I would rather ship fewer pages and have every one hold at 200% zoom than collect effects. That is not a moral stance. It is how you stay hired.

## The last five percent

Hockey had a similar grammar. You do not get credit for the idea of backchecking. You either did it or the other team scored. Front-end is quieter, but the meter is the same: did the person get through, or did they bounce.

The last five percent is rarely a new library. It is the focus ring, the empty state, the print stylesheet, the moment a gust should not move the submit button.

<Code file="lib/wind.ts">
{`export function sample(x: number, y: number) {
  return fbm2(x * 0.02 + time * 0.04, y * 0.02);
}`}
</Code>

One field. One clock. If the type, the video, and the globe disagree about time, the site is a pile of demos. Finish means they agree.
